What do the payroll words mean?

Gross pay

Your pay before deductions. Find the rate and pay period in the offer and paystub. Annual salary and hourly pay are different units.

Net pay

The amount after deductions, often the bank deposit. It does not reveal the gross pay by itself.

Withholding & benefits

Amounts taken for taxes, health insurance or retirement. Ask for each item and who receives it. Withheld tax is not necessarily your final tax bill.

Client billing & employer costs

What a client pays the company and what the company spends. These may be unavailable to you. You do not need them to request pay records or review promised wages.

Paystub access rules vary by state. Federal recordkeeping duties do not alone mean every worker must receive a particular paystub format. Ask the relevant state labor office about your situation.

BEFORE YOU BORROW

A small monthly payment can hide a large cost.

Credit cards

The minimum payment is not a payoff plan. Check the purchase APR, annual fee, promotion end date, and late-payment terms. A true 0% introductory APR and deferred interest are different offers.

The calculator above assumes you keep paying a fixed amount and stop adding purchases.

Explore your card cost ↑

Car loans

Compare the whole deal. Ask for the out-the-door price, amount financed, APR, number of payments, finance charge, and total payments. Check add-ons separately.

A longer term can lower the monthly bill while increasing total interest. Compare offers for the same amount and term.

Auto loan checklist · CFPB ↗

Education loans

Ask what happens while you study. Check fixed versus variable rates, fees, when payments start, whether unpaid interest is added to principal, co-signer obligations, and hardship options.

For a loan in another currency, also consider exchange rates. Ask your school which aid you qualify for; do not assume federal aid eligibility.

APR, interest rate, principal: what is the difference?

Principal is the amount borrowed. Interest is the charge for borrowing it. For a loan, APR incorporates the interest rate and certain fees, so it can differ from the contract interest rate. Compare disclosed APRs with APRs.

The loan tool below needs the contract interest rate and separately entered fee. It does not calculate a disclosed APR or model in-school deferment, variable rates, or currency changes.

Terms to check in the fine print

  • Deferred interest: “No interest if paid in full” can charge accrued interest from the purchase date if the deadline is missed. This calculator models a true introductory APR only.
  • Conditional charges: late/returned-payment fees, penalty APR, cash-advance fees and interest, and foreign-transaction fees depend on the agreement and your actions. They are excluded from this estimate.
  • Loan extras: check optional insurance, add-ons, and prepayment penalties. This model covers a fixed-rate loan with a percentage fee withheld from proceeds; financed or separately paid fees need a different calculation.
Calculation assumptions and sources

Monthly interest rounded to cents, payments at month end, no new spending or missed payments. Card transfer fee is added upfront; annual fee is added in month 1 and every 12 months while a balance remains. Promotions use whole months. Actual dates, minimum transfer fees, fee-specific APRs and variable rates can change results. Loan payment is rounded up with a one-cent cushion to clear rounding residue; APR is not calculated and must not be used as the contract interest rate.
